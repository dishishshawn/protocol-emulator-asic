What the sweep shows

The receiver samples the stop bit 16 clocks after it begins. A fault of 16 clocks or less ends before that sample, so the frame is accepted and the target replies late; from 17 clocks onward the sample sees a low line, the frame is rejected, and the target replies early. That boundary is visible as the single step in the chart, identical for all five bytes.

Everything measured on this page comes from the eight-entry capture FIFO, drained by the host after the run. The engine's own timestamps place every edge one clock after the external edge, the two-stage input synchronizer's latency, which the test checks is constant across all events of a run.

Provenance

Generated by just fault-sweep, which runs test/test_sweep.py under cocotb 2.0.1 and Icarus Verilog 12 against src/engine.v. The firmware is fault_demo(byte, fault_clocks) from tools/streaming.py: one STREAM, CAPTURE on lanes 0 and 4, a UART frame whose stop bit is a low DRIVE of the chosen length followed by a one-clock idle DRIVE, then bounded WAITs for the response edge while the line stays idle. The test asserts, for every run, that the captured events match the independent receiver model's edge list, that the synchronizer offset is constant, that the line measured low from the start of the stop bit for exactly the requested number of clocks, that the receiver's verdict follows from its mid-bit sample, that the response pulse is 11 clocks wide, and that no capture overflow occurred.

The target's response latencies are parameters of the simulation model, chosen so the two outcomes are distinguishable; they are not measurements of any physical device.
